How to write a statement of purpose that gets you admitted

By StudyWork Editorial Team· Reviewed {{UPDATED}}· 7 min read

Admissions readers spend only a few minutes on each statement. Yours needs to answer three questions fast: Why this field? Why this program? Why you, now?

A proven 5-part structure

  1. Hook (80–120 words): a specific moment or problem that sparked your interest.
  2. Academic foundation (150–200 words): relevant courses, projects and results — with numbers.
  3. Practical experience (150–200 words): internships, jobs, research, and what you learned.
  4. Why this program (150 words): named modules, labs, faculty research areas or industry links.
  5. Goals (100–150 words): short- and long-term career goals that the program logically enables.

Weak vs strong

Weak: "I have always been passionate about data science."
Strong: "In my final-year project I cut a local retailer's stock-outs by 18% using a demand-forecasting model — and realised how much I didn't yet know about causal inference."

Final checklist

  • Within the word limit, and every paragraph earns its place
  • Program-specific details (not copy-pasted between universities)
  • Explains gaps or low grades briefly and honestly
  • Proofread aloud; checked by someone else
  • Your own words — AI tools can help edit, but the story and voice must be yours
