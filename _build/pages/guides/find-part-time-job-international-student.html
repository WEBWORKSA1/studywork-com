How to land a part-time job in your first 30 days

By StudyWork Editorial Team· Reviewed {{UPDATED}}· 8 min read

The first month abroad is busy — orientation, housing, a bank account. But students who start their job search early usually find work faster, because they apply before the rush. Here's a practical 30-day plan.

Week 1: paperwork first

  • Get your tax / social insurance number (e.g. SIN in Canada, National Insurance number in the UK, TFN in Australia, PPSN in Ireland).
  • Open a local bank account — most employers pay only into local accounts.
  • Read your visa conditions and note your exact hour limit.
  • Get a local phone number and set up a professional email address.

Week 2: a local-format CV

Many countries expect a different CV style from what you're used to. In Canada, the UK, Australia and Ireland, CVs usually skip photos, date of birth and marital status. Keep it to one or two pages, lead with availability ("available 20 hrs/week in term, full-time in holidays") and highlight customer service, language skills and reliability.

Week 3: apply where students get hired

  1. Your university careers portal and on-campus job boards — employers there expect student schedules.
  2. Walk-in applications to cafés, restaurants and shops with a printed CV (outside peak hours).
  3. Seasonal roles before holidays — retail and logistics hire weeks in advance.
  4. Tutoring and research assistant roles through departments and noticeboards.
  5. StudyWork job alerts — set up your alert.

Week 4: interviews and trial shifts

Prepare three short stories that show reliability, teamwork and problem-solving. Be upfront about your legal hour limits — reputable employers respect them. Unpaid "trial shifts" beyond a short, reasonable trial may be unlawful in some countries; know your rights.

Balancing work and grades

  • Protect exam periods — agree reduced hours in advance.
  • Choose shifts that don't follow late-night work with early lectures.
  • Track hours weekly in a simple spreadsheet so you never exceed your limit.
Your visa depends on your studies. A job that makes you fail a module is a bad trade, however well it pays.
