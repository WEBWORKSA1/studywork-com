Working while studying abroad: the complete 2026 rulebook

By StudyWork Editorial Team· Reviewed September 2026· 9 min read

Part-time work is one of the most searched topics among international students — and one of the most misunderstood. Getting it wrong can cost you your visa. This guide explains, country by country, how many hours you can work, when you can work full-time, and the conditions that usually apply.

Important: this is a summary. Rules change and depend on your individual visa conditions. Always check the official page linked in our country comparison table before you accept a job.

The big picture

Almost every major destination follows the same logic: limited hours during term, more (often full-time) hours during official breaks, and work rights only for full-time students at eligible institutions. What differs is the exact limit and how it is measured — per week, per fortnight, or per year.

  • Per week: Canada (24), UK (20 at degree level), Ireland (20), New Zealand (25)
  • Per fortnight: Australia (48 hours per fortnight during study periods)
  • Per year: Germany (140 full or 280 half days), France (964 hours)
  • On-campus only: USA (F-1 students; off-campus work only via CPT/OPT authorisation)

🇨🇦 Canada

Eligible students can work up to 24 hours per week off-campus during regular academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks. You generally need to be a full-time student at a Designated Learning Institution (DLI), in a program of at least six months leading to a degree, diploma or certificate, with the work condition on your study permit and a Social Insurance Number (SIN). On-campus work and co-op terms have separate rules.

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

Student visa holders studying at degree level can usually work up to 20 hours per week during term (10 hours below degree level) and full-time during official vacations. Restrictions apply: you cannot be self-employed, work as a professional sportsperson or entertainer, or fill a permanent full-time vacancy.

🇦🇺 Australia

Student visa (subclass 500) holders can work up to 48 hours per fortnight while their course is in session and unlimited hours during scheduled course breaks. Work you do as a registered part of your course doesn't count toward the limit.

🇺🇸 United States

F-1 students may work on campus up to 20 hours per week while school is in session and full-time during breaks. Off-campus work requires specific authorisation: Curricular Practical Training (CPT) during studies, or Optional Practical Training (OPT) — usually after graduation. Unauthorised off-campus work is a serious status violation.

🇩🇪 Germany

Non-EU students can work 140 full days or 280 half days per year. Student assistant jobs (studentische Hilfskraft) at the university are often treated separately. Watch the social-insurance and tax thresholds, especially for "mini-jobs".

🇮🇪 Ireland

Stamp 2 students on eligible full-time courses can work 20 hours per week during term and 40 hours per week from 1 June to 30 September and 15 December to 15 January.

🇳🇿 New Zealand

From November 2025, eligible student visa holders can work up to 25 hours per week during term (up from 20), and full-time during scheduled holidays.

🇫🇷 France

Students with a valid student residence permit can work up to 964 hours per year — about 60% of full-time — spread across term and holidays as they choose.

Five mistakes that put visas at risk

  1. Counting hours wrongly across two jobs — limits are combined, not per employer.
  2. Starting work before your course begins — in most countries work rights start with your studies.
  3. Treating gig work as a loophole — self-employment is restricted in several countries.
  4. Working full-time in a "break" that isn't official — only scheduled institutional breaks count.
  5. Keeping no records — keep payslips and timesheets; you may need them later.

What can you actually earn?

At legal term-time limits and local minimum wages, part-time work typically covers a portion of living costs rather than tuition. Use our earnings estimator to model your exact city and hours.

Rule of thumb: plan to fund tuition and at least your first 3–6 months of living costs from savings, scholarships or loans. Treat part-time income as a bonus, not the foundation.
